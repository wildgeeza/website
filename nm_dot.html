02 / The challenge

Flexibility without shader sprawl.

The visual direction called for glowing panels, damaged metal, animated circuitry, and reactive accents. Building each effect independently would increase maintenance cost and make iteration slower for the art team.

The technical challenge was to expose enough artistic control while keeping the shader readable, reusable, and mindful of runtime cost.

Design requirements
  • Shared parameter layout across material instances.
  • Independent control of masks, emissive response, and animation.
  • Artist-friendly defaults and clear naming conventions.
  • Scalable feature complexity for different asset budgets.

03 / Technical approach

From idea to material system.

Step 01

Build the foundation

Established a master material with organized parameter groups for base colour, surface response, emission, and animation.

Step 02

Layer the effects

Combined masks, scrolling patterns, edge response, and damage logic into modular functions that could be enabled independently.

Step 03

Validate in context

Tested the system on multiple meshes and lighting conditions, then refined defaults and exposed controls based on artist feedback.

What I learned

The strongest technical art systems make the right things easy for artists. Clear parameter design, sensible defaults, and early testing in real production contexts are as important as the underlying shader implementation.
